# Photorealistic 3D Animation – OM4 LC Uniboot Polarity Change

## Project goal

Create a photorealistic 3D animation of a real LC Uniboot OM4 multimode fiber optic connector that demonstrates its polarity-change mechanism.

This project must be based on the uploaded reference photos of the actual cable and connector.

The result must look like a realistic product demonstration of the exact connector shown in the photos — not a generic fiber model and not a simplified technical visualization.

---

## Main objective

Build a highly accurate 3D model of the specific LC Uniboot OM4 connector shown in the reference images, then animate the polarity-change process.

The connector must visually match the real photographed connector as closely as possible, including:

- Aqua / turquoise outer housing
- White strain-relief / rear boot
- Aqua outer cable jacket
- Duplex LC connector heads
- “OPEN” text and arrow on the back housing
- Green indicator ring / marker around one connector head
- Internal channels / tracks where the heads sit and slide
- White / transparent and yellow inner fiber paths
- Metallic round shaft / ferrule-retention component at the cable entry
- Overall proportions, surface finish, shape, and small mechanical details

The polarity-change mechanism is different from the previous project and must be modeled specifically from these new reference photos.

---

## Reference material

The `references/` folder contains many photos of the actual connector, including:

1. External views from multiple angles
2. Close-up detail views
3. Back side showing the “OPEN” marking and arrow
4. Opened connector views showing internal structure
5. Polarity-change steps
6. Views showing the internal yellow and white/transparent fiber paths
7. Views showing the metallic round shaft and rear cable-retention area
8. Views showing how the LC heads sit in channels and how one side has a green marker that changes side after polarity change

These reference images are the primary source of truth for geometry, materials, mechanism, and animation logic.

---

## Required workflow

### Phase 1 – Analyze and reconstruct the connector
Before animating, inspect all reference photos and build a detailed 3D model of the connector.

The model should match the real connector as closely as possible:
- shape
- proportions
- small molded details
- colors
- internal structure
- polarity-change logic

### Phase 2 – Validation renders
Before creating the final animation, render preview stills for review.

Required preview renders:
1. Closed connector – hero angle
2. Closed connector – opposite side
3. Rear / back view showing the “OPEN” mark and arrow
4. Open connector showing internal mechanism
5. Open connector from top view showing inner channels and fiber paths
6. Open connector showing the green marker and both LC heads clearly
7. A sequence frame or still showing the polarity-changed state

Do not proceed directly to final MP4 until the previews are reviewed.

### Phase 3 – Final animation
After approval of the model, create the final polarity-change animation.

---

## Animation requirements

Create a clean photorealistic product-style clip showing the polarity-change process.

Suggested sequence:

1. Start with the connector closed in a clean hero view.
2. Hold briefly so the viewer can see the cable clearly.
3. Show the rear housing / latch area and the “OPEN” text / arrow.
4. Open the outer shell / housing smoothly.
5. Reveal the internal structure and channels.
6. Show the two LC heads and the green indicator marker.
7. Demonstrate the polarity-change process according to the reference photos.
8. Make it clear that the green-marked side changes position after the polarity change.
9. Show the internal yellow and white/transparent mini-fiber paths accurately.
10. Show the heads settling back into their proper channels.
11. Close the housing again.
12. End on a clean final shot of the connector in the new polarity state.

---

## Visual quality requirements

The animation must be photorealistic and physically believable.

Required quality:
- Realistic plastic materials
- Slight molded texture where appropriate
- Correct reflections and roughness
- Soft studio lighting
- Natural contact shadows
- Clean edges and realistic bevels
- Accurate part fit and mechanical movement
- No floating geometry
- No clipping through parts
- No exaggerated motion
- No unrelated objects
- No unnecessary text overlays

The final result should resemble a high-quality product demonstration of a real fiber optic connector.

---

## Important accuracy points

Pay special attention to the following:

- The exact shape of the housing
- The “OPEN” text and arrow on the back
- The green indicator ring / marker and its changed side after polarity change
- The head channels and how the LC heads are positioned inside them
- The yellow and white/transparent internal mini-fibers
- The metallic round shaft at the cable-entry area
- The way the shell opens and closes
- The way the inner parts sit and move
- The final closed appearance after the polarity swap

Do not replace these details with generic approximations if the references show them clearly.
